## Business

### The FTC and eight states sue Toast over fees and cancellations
- What it is: On Oct 29 the FTC and eight states (California, Colorado, Florida, Illinois, Iowa, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Utah) sued Toast, the restaurant software and payments company. The complaint says a $0/month plan hid a higher processing fee, a $0.99 online order fee went to Toast and not the restaurant (Sept 2021 to mid-2023), add-ons were billed without consent, "24/7 support" was not delivered, and cancelling needed written notice and sometimes surprise termination fees. It uses ROSCA, the online subscription law, against business contracts for the first time. Toast denies it and says it will fight.
- Platforms: LinkedIn, X, news
- Numbers: American Banker (Oct 30): the FTC has brought about 50 ROSCA cases, the largest settlement $2.5B. Nation's Restaurant News (Oct 29): nearly 200,000 businesses use Toast; over $6B revenue in 2025, more than 80% from financial services. GuruFocus: shares dipped 1.2% on the day. Covered the same day by Reuters, Boston Globe, Inc., Seeking Alpha, QSR Web and SF Chronicle.
- Examples (opened in this run):
  - https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/why-the-ftc-and-8-states-are-suing-payments-fintech-toast (the allegations, ROSCA history and Toast's response, Oct 30)
  - https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-is-sued-by-the-ftc-over-alleged-hidden-fees-24-7-support-claims (the $0.99 fee dates, 200,000 businesses, $6B revenue)
- Momentum: New
- Angle: TagadaPay on LinkedIn, a plain pricing post that does not name or attack Toast: "Every fee on a TagadaPay statement has a name and a line. [Rate]. [Monthly]. [Cancel: one click, no termination fee]. If a processor's pricing needs a lawyer to read, that is the product." Fill the brackets only with confirmed terms.
- Shelf life: 1 to 2 weeks, back with every filing
