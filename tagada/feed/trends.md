# Trends, Nov 2, 2026

Method: trend trackers (Postdom's TikTok page for Nov 2 to 8, updated Nov 2; startups.co.uk's November trends, Nov 2; Know Your Meme's newest entries and the entries for "You're Walking Down A Hallway" (added Nov 1), "Her Curls" (added Oct 29) and "Remember November 2026 Is Coming"), trade press (Social Media Today's latest list, Modern Retail's homepage, Payments Dive's homepage, DTC Newsletter's Oct 26 to 30 issues), and news search (Yahoo Finance, The Next Web, DesignRush, PR Newswire, Shelby Report). Every candidate was checked against this file's git history and the do-not-repeat list. TikTok videos were found on discover pages (`tiktok.com/discover/...`) in Playwright on Nov 2 and opened one by one for plays, likes, comments, shares and follower counts from `__UNIVERSAL_DATA_FOR_REHYDRATION__`. X posts were read through X's syndication endpoint. Dropped for lack of evidence or age: X's Video Kickstart Bonus (announced Oct 16, enrollment closed Oct 30), the Nihilist Penguin (a January meme), "Money can't buy happiness" (biggest posts from September), Bow Bow Bow (biggest posts from September), "Teeny tiny message" (no videos found), Pilothouse's carousels-beat-Reels test (no numbers published) and Threads' custom like buttons (a test in three countries, no numbers). Gaps: reddit.com returned 403, so there is no Reddit data; YouTube pages returned no title or view data, so YouTube numbers are Know Your Meme's; Instagram's embed page for @midihealth showed no posts; Fierce Healthcare returned 403; no Threads or LinkedIn data could be read without a login; the TikTok discover pages for "teeny tiny message", "what ipad", "midi health menopause roast", the Ro Barkley ads and supplement counterfeits returned no videos; the payments stories have little social engagement (the FT's Global Payments post had 35 likes), so they are news trends, not social ones.

### The Fed loses its debit swipe-fee cap in court
- What it is: On Oct 26, a three-judge Eighth Circuit panel upheld the 2025 ruling that the Fed's debit fee cap (Regulation II, from the Durbin Amendment) was set wrongly: the cap should cover only per-transaction costs, not fixed bank costs. The case was brought by Corner Post, a North Dakota truck stop. Merchant groups cheered; bank groups said they were "deeply disappointed" and that it "is not the last word". The current cap stays in place until the court issues its formal mandate and the Fed sets a new rate.
- Platforms: LinkedIn, X, news
- Numbers: Payments Dive (Oct 26): the cap is 21 cents + 0.05% + a 1-cent fraud add-on, set in 2011, for banks with $10B+ in deposits; the Fed's 2023 proposal to cut it to 14.4 cents is still pending. Shelby Report (Oct 27): FMI welcomed the ruling. Via syndication: @AmericasCUs' "BREAKING" post had 3 likes (Oct 26), so it is a trade story, not a social one.
- Examples (opened in this run):
  - https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/fed-loses-debit-card-cap-appeal/872785/ (the ruling, the cap numbers, what's next)
  - https://x.com/AmericasCUs/status/2114816344495395319 (the credit-union reaction, Oct 26, via syndication)
- Momentum: New
- Angle: Skip: news plus "what this means for your store", and nothing changes for merchants until the Fed acts, so any fee line would be a guess.
- Shelf life: Comes back with each Fed step; weeks to months

### Supplement shoppers worry about fakes
- What it is: Modern Retail (Nov 2) on counterfeit supplements sold online. An iHerb survey with YouGov found most online supplement buyers worry about fakes. SuppCo lab-tested top-selling Amazon supplements and found half had under 3% of the advertised active ingredient ("capsules full of rice flour"). Brands answer with proof: Now Foods tests its own and rivals' products, and Ritual points to its clear capsule.
- Platforms: LinkedIn, news (no social numbers found yet)
- Numbers: Modern Retail (Nov 2): 54% of online supplement shoppers are concerned about counterfeits (2,796 US adults, Sep 17 to 21); SuppCo ran 70+ lab tests on 44 top Amazon supplements in 2025, half had under 3% of the active ingredient, over 75% had dupe listings on Alibaba, and nine products were removed; Amazon says it blocked 99.9%+ of suspected infringing listings in 2025.
- Examples (opened in this run):
  - https://www.modernretail.co/operations/online-supplement-counterfeit-products/ (the survey, the lab tests, the quotes)
- Momentum: New
- Angle: Skip for now: it is a fear story, and Tagada cannot vouch for what is in a capsule. Worth watching for Marcus if a nutra brand's own "we tested ours" post takes off.
- Shelf life: 2 to 4 weeks (gift season for supplements)
