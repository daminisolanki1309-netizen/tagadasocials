	**The goal:** every Tagada post should sound like a specific person talking to one operator, never like a logo. This page breaks down why the last two rounds sounded like a brand, what the writers you like actually do line by line, and the toolkit the v3 copy is written with.
</callout>
## Why the last two rounds felt like a brand
- **Nobody was talking.** The copy explained things *to* operators. It never had an "I" who was there, a time of day or a place. Sean's emails start at "4am this morning" in a pitch-black campsite. Ours started with a rule.
- **It was tidy.** "Some things need a room." "Same drop. Different business." Short, balanced lines that wrap the idea up neatly. They read as AI because every brand can post them and nobody remembers them.
- **Symmetry everywhere.** Outside vs inside, job 1 vs job 2, then vs now. One mirror is fine. Ten in a row turns into a template.
- **No characters.** No Sam vs Logan, no Nonna, no Dad, no Zuck who never calls back. Nothing to root for or laugh at.
- **The rules became the voice.** "No em dashes, no hype, no naming" keeps the copy safe, but it doesn't give it a personality. A rulebook can only tell you what to remove.

## Generalist World (Milly)
- **"Hi 👋 Three years ago, I was doomscrolling LinkedIn jobs."** Personal, dated, a little sad, all in the first line.
- **A visible self-edit:** "Every role felt ~~boring~~ narrow." You can see her think.
- **Mocking quotes of the boring thing:** *"5+ years of experience in \[very specific thing\]."*
- **A cliffhanger in plain words:** "Then something *unusual* happened…"
- **Receipts:** "(Yes, really! Screenshot for proof)" and the actual screenshot.
- **"P.S. I'm an actual human, this is my actual email address."** You replied to this one yourself and said it felt like "a fresh breeze of air across my face." That reaction is the bar for Tagada.
- **Lowercase, slightly rude subject lines:** "wtf is a portfolio career?"

## The toolkit the v3 copy uses
Every post uses at least two of these.
1. **A narrator with a body.** Someone is tired, eating, standing in the corner with one bar of signal. "We" is fine if it sounds like a group chat, not a press office.
2. **A timestamp or a place.** 9:14am. Nov 24. The office corner with bad signal.
3. **Make the system talk.** The fraud model, the risk team, the checkout, the decline screen, Black Friday itself. Give them lines and let them be dumb, smug or needy.
4. **One everyday metaphor, stretched.** A bouncer, a houseguest, a parking meter, the college jeans, a restaurant card machine. Never "engine," "stack" or "ecosystem."
5. **Parody a tired format.** Layoff memo, job ad perks, pre-flight safety briefing, out-of-office, a diary, a weather report, terms and conditions.
6. **Say the reader's objection in their voice.** "I can hear you: 'my checkout's fine, we tested it in March.'"
7. **Roast ourselves first.** The team's bad habits, the person who always says "small change," the office plant.
8. **Odd, exact numbers,** and only real ones. 61.6%, not "most."
9. **Recurring cast.** Team members by first name (with their OK), Loïc's existing characters (Derek, Janelle, Theo, Dana), and the personified risk team.
10. **Lists where every item is a joke.**
11. **A parenthetical aside to someone who isn't there.** "(Sorry \[name\].)"
12. **End on a laugh or a small invite,** never a tidy moral.

## Guardrails that stay
- **No slurs, no punching down.** Shock is fine, but the target is the situation or the system, never a group of people.
- **Never invent a customer, a quote or a result.** Where a post needs a real story, it has a slot in \[square brackets\] and a question for the team below.
- **The brand account doesn't swear.** Team members' own accounts can be looser if that's how they already post.
- **Processor names** (Stripe, PayPal, Shopify) stay out of brand copy until the CMO decides. Named versions are marked.
- **Your writing rules still apply.** No em dashes, no exclamation points. The examples you shared use "YESSS" and "!!", so if you want to loosen this for Instagram and team accounts, say so and the skill will allow it there.
## Ask the team on Monday (this is where the real gold is)
These answers turn placeholders into Sean-level stories. Ask in the team chat and paste the replies into the posts.
1. What's the most ridiculous decline reason you've ever seen?
2. What time was the latest a merchant messaged in a panic, and what happened next? (Only with the merchant's OK.)
3. Who on the team always says "small change, won't take long"?
4. What does everyone eat during peak prep?
5. Who has the most open browser tabs right now? How many?
6. What's the funniest thing a processor's risk team ever wrote in an email?
7. Who was the first hire, and what was the office like then?
8. What's the one thing you've each bought online and then checked which processor it used?
9. Who's tallest / most different from what people expected when you met in person?
10. What's the in-joke in the team chat this month?
