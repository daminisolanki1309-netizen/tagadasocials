## Four options (each one sits under the screenshot)
All under 280 characters. The picture does the showing, the words say why it matters.
**1. The boring screen** ⭐ first pick
```plain text
The most boring screen in Tagada.

It's a list of processors. It's also what decides whether a sale survives the first "no."
```
*Why it works:* it admits what the screen looks like, which is the hook, then gives the one operational reason it matters. Same plain-sentence-plus-image pattern as the [Mercury post](https://x.com/mercury/status/2049158529504944158) (32x typical), with the dry self-awareness of the [Ramp offsite joke](https://x.com/tryramp/status/2089917759580741668) (5.8x).
**2. Expensive when it's empty**
```plain text
This is the fallback screen.

When processor one says no, this decides who gets asked next.

Small screen. Expensive when it's empty.
```
*Why it works:* names the screen, then the cost of ignoring it, in a line an operator will quote back.
**3. The second yes**
```plain text
The second yes lives here.

Customer pays. First processor declines. The next one on this list gets a shot at the same sale.
```
*Why it works:* starts using "the second yes" as Tagada's own phrase (see [Notion page](https://app.notion.com/p/3f6e983412b8810a9959d4dc6202e225), X2). If the team likes it, it repeats across posts.
**4. Four steps and a screenshot**
```plain text
Customer pays.
Declined.
Next processor.
Another shot at the sale.

Step three is this screen.
```
*Why it works:* a feature told as a short sequence, like the [Clay post](https://x.com/clay/status/2044476064848605210). Readable in two seconds, and it explains the screen without a single feature word.
