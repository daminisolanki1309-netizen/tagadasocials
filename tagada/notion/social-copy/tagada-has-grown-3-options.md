## How Schwartz would write it
The market is tired of being told a payments company is "trusted." At that stage Schwartz says stop making the claim and let the reader recognise themselves first. So each version does the same three things:
- **Opens a loop on the cover** and closes it later, so people keep swiping.
- **Makes \$1B+ human.** A big number alone says "large company." A big number tied to someone awake at 2am says "someone will pick up."
- **Ends on one easy action** that doubles as a talent filter.

## Which one to pick
- **Best all-rounder: Option A**, because it reads like the team and keeps the brief's best lines ("whoever woke up first").
- **Best for talent: Option C.** Its last slide (a specific question instead of "say hi") also works on A, and I'd steal it.
- **Best for swiping to the end: Option B.** Slide 2 gives the reader a scene before any claim.
## Before it goes live
- **Changed from the brief:** the typo "We'd lying if we said..." is gone. The brief's cover said "overnight" and slide 2 then said it wasn't, so none of these versions use "overnight" on the cover.
- **Numbers:** the team confirms the wording of \$1B+ (what "processed" covers) and 50+ (employees only, or contractors too), and the country count.
- **Names and photos:** real names, real photos, and written OK from each person on the faces slide. The three one-liners are from the brief, so confirm each fits the person.
- **Founder lines:** "founders who'd had their own payouts frozen" and "\[Founder\] still answers the DMs" are founder-attributed. Per the calendar template, set Sensitivity to something other than None so it goes through the sign-off step.
- **Someone has to answer:** all three end on "we read everything," so a named person needs to reply to the comments and DMs.
