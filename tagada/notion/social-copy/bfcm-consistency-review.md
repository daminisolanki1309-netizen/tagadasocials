**In short:** all five headlines are meant to be imperatives, but two break (3 and 5). The bodies are the bigger problem. Three different shapes appear: imperative only (#2), imperative then question (#3, #5), and statement then imperative (#4).
## The pattern
Every content slide gets the same three lines.
- **Headline:** an imperative with a real action verb.
- **Do:** one imperative sentence.
- **Pass if:** one statement that says what good looks like. No questions anywhere.
That matches the cover, which calls these "checks": a check has a task and a pass mark.

## What changed and why
- "Get declined on purpose" became "Fail a payment on purpose" (active verb, same idea).
- "Be a 1 AM shopper" became "Test it at 1 AM" (an action, still has the time-of-night hook).
- "Stack like a pro" became "Stack every offer" (says what to do).
- "Review your fraud rules" became "Stress-test your fraud rules" so it reads like the other four tests.
- The three-item instruction on #1 became two steps. The three-item list on #2 stays because it is a real list.
- Every question became a "Pass if" line. If you prefer questions, flip all five to "Can ...?" and keep every other line as written.
- The CTA no longer says "fix this". It turns the point into a line people repeat: run the tests before the traffic does.
- The #1 stat is now small text so no slide ends on a statement. It is Adobe's figure for Thanksgiving Day 2025, via [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/article/thanksgiving-ecommerce-sales/).
## Two things for the team
- **Optional second stat for #4:** fraud ran about 1.7 times normal over Black Friday to Cyber Monday last year, per [Stripe's peak season guide](https://stripe.com/guides/preparing-for-peak-shopping-season-2026). It would match #1 as small text. Needs a team OK before use.
- **Date:** "before Black Friday" is Nov 27. Stripe says volume turned up around Nov 17 last year, so if the post goes out close to the date, "before Nov 17" is the safer deadline.
