## The screen
- **What it shows:** the Payment Flow edit screen with a toggle ("Enable Fallback: retry with alternative processors on failure"), a Max retries box set to 2, and a Fallback queue where backup processors go.
- **Why it matters operationally:** when one processor declines, the payment can be retried with another one instead of ending as a lost sale. That is the whole "keep selling when a processor says no" idea, on one screen.
- **Source:** [Tagada help center, Activate Fallback Settings](https://help.tagada.io/en/articles/32-activate-fallback-settings). Product background: [Payments Feature Explained](https://help.tagada.io/en/articles/35-payments-feature-explained).
- **For the designer:** use the Fallback Processors card only. Crop out the red tutorial arrows, the browser bar and the "Milena Tagada V2" account name. Ideally capture it fresh from a demo store with real processor names in place of "New processor" (naming processors needs the CMO's OK).
## Option 1: Say what it is
Benefit first, then the screen, then the how. The most direct version of the brief.
```plain text
A decline doesn't have to end the sale.

This is the Fallback Processors panel. Switch it on, set max retries (2 here), and the next processor in your queue gets a go when the first one says no.
```

## Before it goes live
- **Screen check:** the screenshot is from the current dashboard (help center, early 2026). V3 is on the calendar, so someone on the product team should confirm this panel looks the same today and is not part of V3.
- **Wording check:** the help center says fallback retries a failed payment with an alternative processor, and the Payment Flows list shows "2x Retries." The payments team should confirm that "max retries 2" means up to two further attempts and that the queue is tried in order.
- **Option 4:** "Nobody opens a laptop" assumes the retry is fully automatic. Confirm, or cut that line.
- **Naming:** no processor names are used in any option. Keep it that way until the CMO decides.
- **No customer-facing claims:** none of the options say what the shopper sees at checkout, on purpose.
