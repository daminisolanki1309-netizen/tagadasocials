	**The question:** if Eugene Schwartz, Gary Bencivenga and Gary Halbert were alive today, with X search, Reddit, the Meta Ad Library, Claude and a phone in their pocket, how would they approach Tagada's social, and what would they write? This page is their method. The copy itself is in [Notion page](https://app.notion.com/p/3f6e983412b8810a9959d4dc6202e225). Every tool named here is free (or SortIn Pro, which you already use).

## The short answer
All three would say the same uncomfortable thing first: **the last three rounds of copy were written from the desk, not from the market.** We tuned the voice. They would have tuned the *idea* first.
- **Schwartz** would find the one fear Tagada's buyers already lose sleep over, work out how many times the market has heard the promise, and give Tagada's mechanism a name people can repeat.
- **Bencivenga** would refuse to post anything Tagada can't prove, then bury the reader in proof so specific it reads like a court exhibit.
- **Halbert** would ignore "brand" entirely, find the operators who are bleeding *right now* (the starving crowd), and send each of them something so personal and useful they can't not reply.
Then all three would write less and say more. Fewer posts about team culture and calendars. More posts that make an operator stop scrolling because it's about *their money, this week*.

## 1. Eugene Schwartz: channel the desire that's already there
**What he believed.** "Copy cannot create desire for a product. It can only take the hopes, dreams, fears and desires that already exist in the hearts of people" and focus them on your product ([Breakthrough Advertising, via Taylor Pearson](https://taylorpearson.me/bookreview/breakthrough-advertising/)). Choosing that one mass desire is the most important decision in the whole campaign. Then you work out how aware the reader is, and how many times the market has already heard the claim.
**How he'd research today (all free):**
- **X advanced search** for the market's own words: `"payouts on hold"`, `"account under review"`, `"reserve" shopify`, `"do not honor"` filtered to posts with 20+ likes. He'd read 300 of them before writing a word. SortIn Pro to save the best.
- **Reddit** (r/shopify, r/ecommerce, r/dropship, r/smallbusiness) and **Shopify Community** threads sorted by most replies. The longest threads are where the desire is hottest.
- **1-star Trustpilot reviews of every processor and checkout tool.** Complaints are desires turned inside out.
- **Google Trends** for "shopify payments hold" and "stripe account frozen" to see when the fear spikes.
- **AnswerThePublic and AlsoAsked** (free tiers) for the exact questions people type.
- **Meta Ad Library** for every competitor's long-running ads, to measure how sophisticated the market is (what's been promised, how many times).
- **Claude** to cluster 1,000 complaints into themes and count which fear shows up most. He wrote with scissors and glue. Today the glue is a spreadsheet.

## 2. Gary Bencivenga: belief is the whole game
**What he believed.** The reader's skepticism is the real enemy, so proof does most of the selling. His widely quoted persuasion equation: **Urgent problem + Unique promise + Unquestionable proof + User-friendly proposition.** He made his claims smaller than the truth so the reader would believe all of it, and he wrote "fascinations," bullets so specific and curious that the reader has to know the answer.
**How he'd research today (all free):**
- **Tagada's own data first.** Decline codes by frequency, saved orders per month, approval lift by merchant, median payout time, how fast support answers. One real internal number beats ten industry stats.
- **Public proof Tagada already has:** a merchant's 72.0% to 83.9% approvals over six months "and they changed nothing" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7501672811438960640/)), \$1.12M in 30 days with \$22K of refunds shown (Instagram), Trustpilot reviews from Théo, Franck, Justin Roe and Nicky ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tagada.io)).
- **Third-party facts with links:** Visa's "Excessive" VAMP threshold for merchants dropped from 2.2% to 1.5% on April 1, 2026 in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific, counting fraud reports and disputes together ([Fraud.net](https://fraud.net/resources/visas-vamp-thresholds-drop-to-1-5-on-april-1-are-you-ready)). PayPal's user agreement allows holds of up to 180 days. Fraud ran about 1.7x normal over BFCM 2025 and volume turned up around Nov 17 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/guides/preparing-for-peak-shopping-season-2026)).
- **Live demonstrations:** Loom screen recordings of a real decline being saved, Microsoft Clarity recordings of checkouts (free), Chrome DevTools phone mode to test any checkout on a "phone."
- **Wayback Machine** to see how competitors' claims changed over time, which shows what stopped working.

## 3. Gary Halbert: find the starving crowd and write them a letter
**What he believed.** "To beat all of you, all I need is a starving crowd." The market matters more than the copy. Then make it personal: plain envelopes that look like a letter from a friend, a "grabber" in the envelope (he stapled a real \$1 bill to letters), and his family coat of arms letter, personalised with the reader's own surname, which was mailed over a billion times ([Profit Advisors](https://www.profitadvisors.com/halbert.shtml)). Emotion first, logic second, and write like you talk.
**How he'd work today (all free):**
- **Find the starving crowd live.** Saved X searches for "payouts on hold," "account under review," "shopify payments hold," checked every hour. **F5Bot** for free email alerts when those words appear on Reddit. Google Alerts for news. Every one of those posts is a person with an urgent, expensive problem right now.
- **The A-pile.** On social, the A-pile is the DM and the reply. A thoughtful human reply under a panicked post beats any brand post. Halbert would have Eden or someone from support reply within the hour, helping first and selling never.
- **Coat of arms at scale.** Clay (free tier) plus BuiltWith lookups to find stores on a given platform, then Claude to write a genuinely personal note per store: their store name, their product, something true about their checkout. Personalisation used to cost a printing press. Now it costs an afternoon.
- **The grabber.** The modern \$1 bill is something useful made just for them: a screen recording of someone buying from their store on a phone, or their decline email translated into plain English.
- **Letters, not posts.** His best work was a letter from one person. On LinkedIn and X that means long posts from Eden and Loïc in first person, with the warts left in.
**His diagnosis:** Tagada's starving crowd posts in public every day and nobody from Tagada is in those replies. That's the cheapest, fastest growth channel available, and it also produces the stories every other post needs (with permission).

<tr>
<td>**1. The Freeze Count**</td>
<td>Bencivenga's proof plus Louis Grenier's counting</td>
<td>Publicly count the money operators say is frozen, using their own public posts (with links, and with permission to feature them). Starts at \$1.3M from three posts already in our Voice of Customer table. Update it monthly.</td>

<td>Schwartz's named mechanism</td>

<tr>
<td>**3. Send Us The Email** (team time)</td>
<td>Halbert's grabber and useful offer</td>
<td>Operators DM a screenshot of the "under review" or "payouts on hold" email. A human replies with what it means and what to do first, whether or not they ever use Tagada. With permission, anonymised versions become "The email, decoded" carousels.</td>
<td>Free, human, immediately useful at the worst moment of someone's month. People screenshot the reply and share it.</td>
</tr>

<td>**4. We Bought From Your Store** (team time)</td>
<td>Halbert's coat of arms</td>

<td>A personal grabber at scale. Every recording is a reason to reply, and the anonymised patterns become a "we bought from 50 stores" report.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>**5. The Black Friday Log**</td>
<td>Halbert's candour plus Schwartz's timing</td>
<td>On Nov 27, live-post from the payments team, hour by hour: decline spikes, fixes, snacks. Announce it two weeks before.</td>
<td>Operators will have the tab open all day to compare with their own numbers. We haven't found another payments company doing it.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>**6. Hold Survivors**</td>
<td>Schwartz's identification plus Instagram's collab outlier</td>
<td>Collab posts with real operators who lived through a freeze, told in their words. Posted on both accounts.</td>
<td>Collabs are the biggest outlier format for fintech on Instagram, and every story is the mass desire with a face.</td>
</tr>

## 5. What they'd tell the team on Monday
- **Bring me numbers.** Saved orders last month, approval lift by merchant, median time to first support reply, most common decline code. With permission to publish.
- **Bring me one real freeze story per founder.** Eden's suspension, in full, with times and amounts.
- **Fix the homepage proof** before any campaign sends traffic there (stats that read as zero, placeholder testimonial photos).
- **Decide who answers** the "Send us the email" DMs and the live X searches, and how fast.
- **Decide on naming** Stripe, PayPal and Shopify. Halbert and Schwartz would name the villain. The swipe file says "your processor" wherever it can, and flags the quotes that name a platform.
