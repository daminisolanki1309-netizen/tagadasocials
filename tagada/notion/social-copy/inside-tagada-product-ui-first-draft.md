## Option 1: The plain one (benefit, how, proof)
Built on the Mercury CLI post: one plain sentence plus a clean product image, no hype words.
```plain text
This screen decides what happens when a processor says no.

Your processors, in the order a payment tries them. The first one declines, the next one gets a shot at the same sale.

Stores with one processor never see it. They also never need it, right up until they do.
```
*Why it works:* the screenshot is the proof, the copy only says what to look at. The last line is the dry "calm under fire" note, aimed at the situation and not at anyone.
## Option 2: The five-step mood
Built on Clay's "build → save → break → revert → relax" post, where a feature is told as a feeling.
```plain text
Sale → decline → next processor → approved → relax

The third step lives on this screen. You choose who gets asked next.
```
*Why it works:* shortest version, readable in two seconds on a feed, and it explains the screen without a single feature word. If it runs from Eden's or Loïc's own account, it can go lowercase.
## Option 3: The calendar hook
Uses the real date (Black Friday is Nov 27) so the screen has a reason to be seen this week.
```plain text
Black Friday is Nov 27. Know what this screen says before then.

It's the order your processors get asked in. If the first one says no on the busiest day of your year, the second one still gets a go at the sale.
```
*Why it works:* operational and urgent without fear. It tells the reader what to check, which is what makes a product post worth saving.
## Option 4: The open door (trust and talent)
For the "show we're open to good people" part of the ask. It invites operators to critique the screen, which signals confidence, and the last line says who we want to hear from.
```plain text
This is the live screen where you set the order your processors get tried in.

If you'd have built it differently, say so in the replies. We read them, and we're hiring people who have opinions about checkouts.
```
*Why it works:* showing the actual product instead of describing it is a trust move on its own, and asking for criticism makes it a conversation. Replies add reach.
⚠️ Needs the team to confirm that we are hiring right now and what to link, and that someone will actually read and answer the replies.
