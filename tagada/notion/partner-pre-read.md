# Opening · Agencies
**Own your clients' payment setup, and earn on every store you bring.**
You already build your clients' funnels and write their emails. Then the money goes through a processor and a pile of tools you don't control, and you earn nothing on any of it.
Tagada lets you sell the whole setup as your own product. Your clients run their checkout, funnels, subscriptions and payments on one platform with your name on it. You set what they pay for the platform, and the markup is yours.
Your clients also own their saved cards and subscribers. If they ever leave, that data goes with them, which takes the biggest fear out of switching. «Product: confirm portability wording.»
«Optional line for agencies that serve health or peptide brands: The main Rx platforms don't run partner programs. Tagada Rx does, so you can offer telehealth to your clients and earn on it. Milena to confirm before use.»
Everything below is the same for every partner. Read it before the call, and we'll spend the call on your clients instead of a product tour.
# Opening · ISOs and agents
**Place the high-risk merchants other programs turn down. Earn without a processing buy rate.**
You know how it goes. A merchant with real volume sells peptides or supplements, and half your processors pass before they open the file. The ones that say yes cap the volume or ask for a reserve big enough to scare the merchant off.
Tagada boards those merchants when the business behind them checks out. Every file goes through underwriting, and the merchant's risk tier decides what they need to go live. We can't promise approval, and we'll tell you early when a merchant isn't a fit. And a merchant who joins keeps their saved cards and subscribers if they ever leave, so nobody signs up feeling trapped.
Your margin also doesn't live on a few basis points. You earn on the platform your merchants run their store on, so there's no processing buy rate to fight over. «Milena: confirm this applies to ISOs on every model, or only on white label.»
Everything below is the same for every partner. Read it before the call, and we'll spend the call on your merchants instead of a product tour.
# Opening · PSPs

# Same for every partner
## 1. What Tagada is
**One platform your merchants run their store on, with your brand on top.**
Tagada is two products that work as one.
- **Tagada CRM** is where the merchant runs the store: checkout, funnels, subscriptions and every customer record.
- **Tagada Pay** is the payment processing underneath it.
Partners can put their own brand on both. «Confirm what white label covers: dashboard, merchant login, domain.»
**Tagada Pay only works through Tagada CRM.** A merchant can't take Tagada Pay on its own and plug it into another checkout or CRM. They move onto the platform, and the platform is what you sell and earn on.
**Your merchants own their customer vault.** Saved cards and subscribers belong to the merchant, and they can take them along if they ever leave their processor or Tagada. When a merchant is scared of being cut off, this is the line that settles it. «Product: confirm portability wording.»

# Notes for Tess and Milena (not part of the doc)
## What the competitor research changed
From [Partnerships: What We Found](https://claude.ai/artifact/Jt59TwjEPdWZiEhNjmLP3i) (8 Oct).
- **Vault portability now leads.** The research says it's the one thing only Tagada can claim and the first thing partners should hear. It's now in the agency and ISO openings and as its own line in section 1.
- **The agency opening sells the switch to the agency as well as the product.** The research found agencies decide which platform a merchant runs on, so the agency has to come out ahead too.
- **Referral is the current 10% affiliate program.** The research lists Tagada's referral payout as 10% lifetime, which matches the public affiliate page. The doc shows the structure (a share of fees, for as long as the merchant pays) and leaves the rate for the call. That matters here: 10% is the bottom of a 10% to 35% market, and the research says a 20% to 30% rate is being considered.
- **A placeholder for what partners get beyond commission.** CheckoutChamp pays only 10% but wins agencies with certification, a partner dashboard, sales scripts, a directory listing and coaching. Section 6 now has a slot for Tagada's version.
- **An optional Rx line for health and peptide agencies.** The research found the main Rx players run no partner programs. It's marked optional because it rests on two competitors checked.
- **One risk for Milena.** The research warns against sending partners more Rx merchants than Risk can approve. Worth checking underwriting capacity before the Rx partner push.
## Five places this copy disagrees with the public partner page
Partners will read [tagada.io/partners](https://www.tagada.io/partners) next to this doc. I followed the brief. Either the site or the brief needs to change before Oct 19.
1. **Buy rate.** The site says partners get a buy rate and set their own processing price. The brief says white label has no buy rate on processing and the margin comes from the CRM markup. Our approved ISO letter also says partners earn on "processing, checkout and CRM".
2. **Minimum.** The site says "no surprise minimums". The brief has a \$200K a month minimum.
3. **Branding.** The site says merchants never see Tagada. The descriptor shows "Tagada".
4. **Processor choice.** The site says partners can bring their own acquirers and shows "Your MID" in the dashboard. The FAQ says the processor can't be chosen.
5. **Speed and fit.** The site says under 24 hours to live processing, every industry welcome, no volume ceiling. This doc says underwriting and risk tiers decide, with times to confirm.
## Questions only Milena or Loïc can answer
- **The \$200K a month.** In Loïc's pricing note, \$200K a month is the entry point for each Rx brand. The brief lists it as a partner responsibility. Is it a partner minimum, an Rx merchant minimum, or both?
- **Rx in a white-label doc.** Rx is co-branded only, and the Rx ad rules say never to mention white label next to it. I wrote it as co-branded. OK to keep it in this doc?
- **Preferred partner.** I couldn't find a definition anywhere, so that whole column is placeholders.
- **Naming Stripe.** The FAQ names Stripe because the brief does. Named processors usually need CMO sign-off.
- **Onboarding support.** 5 merchants now. Some older calls said 10, so partners may expect 10.
