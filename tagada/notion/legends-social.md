## The short version
All three would start in the same place: they would refuse to write a single post until they had read hundreds of things merchants already say about getting paid. Then each would add one thing.
- **Gary Halbert** would find the hungriest crowd and talk to it like a friend writing a letter. For Tagada that crowd is operators with money on hold right now.
- **Gary Bencivenga** would make every post provable. A screenshot, a clause, a dated number. Belief is the product.
- **Eugene Schwartz** would diagnose the market first. Payments is a tired market where everyone says "all in one", so Tagada has to win with a new mechanism (a second route when one processor says no) and with a reader who feels recognised before they feel sold to.
Put together, that gives Tagada one job on social: **be the account operators check when their money stops moving.**

## 1. Gary Halbert: find the starving crowd, then write to one person
**What he was known for**
- The hamburger stand question he asked students: if you could have one advantage, what would it be? His answer was a starving crowd. Market first, copy second.
- The Boron Letters, written to his son from prison: practical letters on studying markets and writing every day.
- Making mail land in the "A pile" (personal letters people open) instead of the "B pile" (anything that looks like an ad).
- Copying great ads out by hand so the rhythm gets into your fingers.
**How he would build Tagada's social today**
1. **Live where the starving crowd already talks.** X search for `"payouts on hold" min_faves:20`, `"froze my" (stripe OR paypal OR shopify)`, the Shopify Community forums, Trustpilot reviews of every processor, Reddit r/shopify and r/dropship. He would sort by most recent and reply to people in trouble every single day, as a person, with one useful next step. No pitch in the first reply.
2. **Write A-pile posts.** On every platform, the posts that win look like a person talking: Whop's CEO announcing news in one plain sentence (1.07M views), Ryanair's three-word replies, Tyler Denk's numbers. Halbert would ban designed graphics for the first 30 days and make the account sound like Eden.
3. **One letter a week: "Dear Operator".** A short letter on LinkedIn and X from Eden to the merchant whose payouts just got paused. Same opening every week so people recognise it.
4. **Copy out 50 winners by hand.** Before writing anything, he would type out the 50 best posts from the Swipe File word for word, then write 100 hooks of his own.
**How he would build the swipe file**
- Only from people who are bleeding right now. The Voice of Customer table is his swipe file: "Another day another hold", "400k is in the hands of god right now", "We grew too fast", "30% hold... is our entire operating margin".
- Each entry tagged with the exact words, the dollar figure and the emotion, so a writer can lift the language without lifting the person.
**His rule for Tagada:** if a merchant in trouble wouldn't stop scrolling for it, don't post it.

## 2. Gary Bencivenga: make it impossible to doubt
**What he was known for**
- His persuasion equation: urgent problem, plus a unique promise, plus unquestionable proof, plus a proposition that's easy to say yes to.
- A belief that the most believable ad wins, and that proof does more selling than adjectives ever will.
- Writing ads that beat long-running controls in split tests.
**How he would build Tagada's social today**
1. **A proof library before a content calendar.** Every claim Tagada wants to make, with the evidence next to it: the PayPal User Agreement clause allowing holds of up to 180 days, product screenshots of a decline retried on a backup processor, merchant results with written permission, dated approval-rate numbers once the team signs off. If a claim has no proof, it doesn't ship.
2. **Show the receipt.** Coinbase proved stablecoin payments by auctioning a real car (375K views). Whop's CEO posts the monthly number with a ladder of smaller numbers under it. Bencivenga would make Tagada's version a monthly post with real totals.
3. **Remove the risk from the first step.** The offer he would love: "Payouts on hold right now? Send us the email you got. We'll tell you what it means, whether or not you use Tagada." Easy yes, real value, proof of competence.
4. **Test hooks the way he tested headlines.** Same body, five hooks, one variable at a time. Keep the winner, kill the rest. The ad-test discipline already in RMP-41 applies to organic posts too.
**How he would build the swipe file**
- He would add a column most swipe files don't have: **what proof did the post use?** (screenshot, number, named person, document, live demo). Then he would sort by it.
- Example from the new rows: Razorpay's "Save The Cards" film (a funeral for failed card payments) won a Shorty Award and Razorpay reports 754K+ organic impressions. The proof there is the creative itself: everyone has seen a payment fail.
**His rule for Tagada:** never say "reliable". Show the day it was reliable.

## 3. Eugene Schwartz: read the market's awareness and sophistication first
**What he was known for**
- *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966): the five levels of awareness (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware) and five stages of market sophistication.
- "Copy is not written. Copy is assembled." The words come from the market's own desires, gathered first.
- The idea that a copywriter can't create desire, only channel a desire that already exists.
- Working in timed 33-minute sessions.
**How he would diagnose Tagada's market**
- **Sophistication: stage 4 to 5.** Every processor and checkout claims "all in one", "higher approvals", "high-risk friendly". Operators have heard every promise. At stage 4, a new **mechanism** wins. At stage 5, **identification** wins: the reader sees themselves in the post before any claim appears.
- **Tagada's mechanism:** a second route. When one processor says no, a backup processor can retry the sale. It's simple, and you can picture it.
- **Tagada's identification:** the 2am "We're reviewing your account" email. Every operator recognises it.
**How he would spread content across awareness levels**

Most payment brands post almost only at the product-aware level. Schwartz would weight Tagada's mix the other way: most posts unaware and problem aware, because that's where the crowd is.
**How he would build the swipe file**
- He would tag every row by awareness level and sophistication answer (claim, mechanism, identification). The Swipe File already has an Awareness stage column. The 35 new rows are tagged.
- He would keep a separate "desire" sheet: one line per desire the market already has ("I just want to scale without checking if my account still works"), and assemble posts from those lines.
**His rule for Tagada:** don't argue that payments are broken. They already know. Show them you know exactly how it breaks.

## 4. Their combined operating system for Tagada
**Daily, 45 minutes**
1. Search X for operator pain (saved searches above). Reply to five people in trouble, as a person.
2. Log every new phrase in the Voice of Customer table with the link.
3. Check the Watchlist accounts for any post doing 3x their usual numbers. Add it to the Swipe File with why it worked and a Tagada version.
**Weekly**
1. One "Dear Operator" letter (Halbert).
2. One proof post: a screenshot, a clause or a dated number (Bencivenga).
3. One mechanism explainer and one identification post (Schwartz).
4. Five hooks tested on the same body copy across the week.
**Monthly**
1. A scoreboard post with real numbers (only after the team signs off).
2. Retire formats that fell below the account's typical post; double the ones above 3x.
**Free tools they would use today**
- X Advanced Search with `min_faves:` and `since:` to find outliers in minutes.
- Meta Ad Library to see which ads competitors keep running for months (a long-running ad is a winning ad).
- LinkedIn search sorted by top posts, and Instagram's own search for collab posts.
- SortIn Pro for X bookmarks, Notion for the tables, Trustpilot and Shopify Community for unfiltered merchant language.

## 5. Five plays that could make Tagada the talk of the town
1. **The Hold Hotline.** A standing offer on X and LinkedIn: send the hold or review email you got, Tagada explains it in plain English, customer or not. Every answer (anonymised, with permission) becomes a post. ⚠️ Needs a team owner who will answer within 24 hours.
2. **Dear Operator.** A weekly letter from Eden, same opening line, same day. Halbert's A-pile on LinkedIn.
3. **Sale obituaries.** An Instagram and LinkedIn series in the spirit of Razorpay's funeral for failed card payments: "In loving memory of a sale the customer approved and the processor declined." Dry and true. Never names a real merchant.
4. **The monthly scoreboard.** Whop-style: total processed, how many stores crossed \$10K, \$100K and \$1M. ⚠️ Only with numbers the team approves.
5. **One phrase, repeated for a year.** Barry Hott owned "ugly ads" by repeating it. Tagada's phrase: "Keep selling when a processor says no." It goes in every bio, every pinned post and every reply signature.

## 6. Ready-to-post swipes
### X, single posts
**X-1 (problem aware, Bencivenga)**
```plain text
Your customer said yes.
Your processor said no.

You paid for that click either way.
```
Alternate hooks: "Every decline is a sale you already paid to get." / "The customer approved it. The processor didn't."
**X-2 (problem aware, Halbert)**
```plain text
Another day, another hold.

If one company can pause your payouts, one company decides when you get paid.

Set up a second route before you need it.
```
Alternate hooks: "Your store runs on one processor's yes." / "How many days could you pay for ads if your payouts stopped tonight?"
**X-3 (proof, Bencivenga)**
```plain text
Some payment terms allow a provider to hold your balance for up to 180 days.

Half a year of revenue you already shipped against.

Open your provider's user agreement tonight and search for "hold". Takes five minutes.
```
Source: PayPal User Agreement, section on holds ("for up to 180 days if reasonably needed to protect against the risk of liability"). Don't name PayPal in the post until the CMO decides.
**X-4 (unaware, Schwartz identification)**
```plain text
2am. Ads finally profitable. Inbox: "We're reviewing your account."

We call that Tuesday.
```
**X-5 (offer, Halbert and Bencivenga)**
```plain text
Payouts on hold right now?

Reply with the email you got (hide your numbers). We'll tell you in plain English what it means and what to send back.

Whether or not you use Tagada.
```
⚠️ Only post when someone on the team can answer every reply within 24 hours.
### X, thread (solution aware, Schwartz mechanism)
```plain text
1/ Your customer typed their card in perfectly. The sale still died.

Plenty of declines have nothing to do with the buyer. 9 of them, and what each one costs you:

2/ The issuing bank's fraud model flags the order. Common on a store's first big day, when volume looks unusual.

3/ The billing address doesn't match closely enough. One typo in a zip code is enough for some banks.

4/ 3D Secure fails or times out on mobile. The customer never even sees an error they can fix.

5/ Your processor has a few minutes of downtime. Orders that hit in that window just fail.

6/ Your product category is coded as high risk, so some banks decline by default.

7/ Too many attempts in a short window trip a velocity limit.

8/ The card was short on funds today. A retry on payday would have worked, but nobody retried.

9/ The bank returns "Do Not Honor", the code that tells you nothing.

10/ Several of these are soft declines. The same sale can go through on a second attempt or a different route.

That's why a backup processor matters: when one says no, the sale has somewhere else to go. Tagada can retry it there.

Save this for the next time someone tells you "the card was bad".
```
⚠️ Confirm with Loïc that "Tagada can retry it there" matches how retries and payday retries work today.
### LinkedIn
**LI-1, company page (unaware, Surreal-style identification)**
```plain text
If you've never had a payout held, please scroll past.

This post is only for people who have.

You know the email. You know the subject line. You know the exact feeling of checking your bank balance before checking your sales.

You also know nobody calls you back.

We built Tagada so a single "no" from one processor isn't the end of your week. A backup processor can retry the sale, and a real team answers.

If that email is sitting in your inbox right now, comment "hold". Someone on our team will reply personally.
```
⚠️ Needs a named team member ready to reply.
**LI-2, Eden, "Dear Operator" #1 (Halbert letter, SLAY structure)**
```plain text
Dear Operator,

Every day an operator posts some version of "Help me. My account just got suspended."

[Story: one real merchant, with permission. The time it happened, the amount held, what they had already spent on ads that week.]

What it taught us: the hold itself is rarely the thing that kills a store. Not knowing why, and not knowing when the money comes back, is.

Three things to do this week, whoever you process with:
1. Read the holds section of your provider's agreement. Note the maximum hold period.
2. Keep enough cash outside your processor to run ads for two weeks without a payout.
3. Have a second processor approved before you scale, not after.

What did you learn the first time your payouts stopped?

Eden
```
⚠️ Real story only. Eden reviews and edits in his own words.
**LI-3, Eden or a team lead (opinion, Tyler Denk style)**
```plain text
A merchant should never find out about a payout hold from their bank balance.

That's a hill we'll die on at Tagada.

If a payment company holds your money, it owes you three things the same day: the reason, the amount, and the date it ends.

Most merchants get none of them. They get a form and a queue.

Operators: what should a processor owe you before it holds your money?
```
⚠️ Confirm Tagada itself meets this standard before publishing.
### Instagram
**IG-1, carousel: "What a payout hold email actually means"**
1. Slide 1: "You got the email. What it actually means."
2. Slide 2: "We're reviewing your account." Meaning: a risk team is checking unusual volume or disputes. Often triggered by fast growth.
3. Slide 3: "A reserve has been placed." Meaning: a percentage of your sales is kept back for a period of time. Ask for the percentage and the end date in writing.
4. Slide 4: "Your payouts are paused." Meaning: sales still come in, money doesn't go out. Your ad spend keeps going.
5. Slide 5: "Please provide documents." Meaning: send invoices, supplier proof and tracking numbers fast and in one reply.
6. Slide 6: The one thing to do before it happens: have a second processor approved.
7. Slide 7: "Save this. Send it to the person who runs your payments."
⚠️ Ask the risk team to check the meanings on slides 2 to 5.
